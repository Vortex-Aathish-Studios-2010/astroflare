1. Start here

AstroFlare has two tools on one website. Use the research explorer to find out what NASA experiments say. Use mission screening to find out which fire-safety conditions in a mission deserve a specialist's attention.

I want to find NASA evidence

Go to Lunar habitat demo or NASA research. You get three NASA experiments ranked for your question, what each measured, and the exact table row behind every number.

Time needed: two minutes for the demo.

I want to screen a mission

Go to Mission screening. You describe a mission, answer a short guided set of questions, and get a cited list of conditions to review, what is missing, and a report to download.

Time needed: about ten minutes.

Before you begin

  • It is a screening tool, not a safety verdict. It never says a design is safe, never gives a probability, and has not been validated by experiment or by a safety authority. Treat its output as questions for a qualified team.
  • Everything is hypothetical. It is not connected to any spacecraft. Use illustrative numbers if you are exploring.
  • First visit may take about a minute. The free service sleeps when idle; the first page load wakes it. After that it is quick.
  • Nothing is stored about you or your mission. Your answers live in your browser page. They are saved on your device only if you press Save in this browser.

Two-minute tour

  1. Open the site and press Run the lunar habitat demo.
  2. Read the ranked experiments and the line explaining why the first ranked first.
  3. In Ask about the evidence, press What was the flame spread rate in Saffire II?
  4. Read the answer: the number, its conditions, and the link to the original NASA table.
  5. Press Explain with AI (optional) if you want a plain-language explanation. It is labelled as automatically generated.
Where the numbers come from. Every figure in the explorer links to the original NASA table and row. Every finding in mission screening cites recorded statements from the 14 NASA documents in the library. Use those links: they are the point of the tool.

Working with the six stages

The layout adapts to the screen: the menu wraps onto two lines on narrow screens, tables scroll sideways inside their own area, and buttons become larger on touch screens. On a phone held sideways the title and main buttons still fit on screen.

3. The NASA research explorer

Find which NASA experiments can inform your question, compare them, and read exactly what they measured.

3.1 Your research question

The page opens on a question. For the demo it is "Which NASA experiments can inform a review of solid-material flammability in a ventilated lunar habitat?" Open Edit research question, type your own, and press Update ranked evidence. The ranking is recalculated with the same fixed rules; nothing is sent to an AI.

3.2 The filters

FilterChoices
Material / fuelAny solid material; SIBAL / cotton-fiberglass; PMMA; Silicone; Gaseous fuel (coverage check)
Experiment gravityAny gravity; Microgravity; Lunar surface; Earth gravity
Airflow evidenceAny airflow; Forced ventilation; Variable-flow investigation; Documented 20 cm/s; Concurrent-flow investigation; Opposed-flow investigation; Quiescent (coverage check)
Scenario gravity (for transfer limits)Lunar surface; Microgravity; Earth gravity; Unspecified. This sets the gravity your scenario is about, so the page can warn when an experiment was done in a different gravity.

The filters are hard rules, not preferences. If a filter leaves no matching experiment, the page says so and shows Reset experiment filters. A short sentence under the filters explains what changed each time you change one. Restore lunar preset returns to the demo's original question and settings.

3.3 The ranked results

Each experiment appears as a result with a Why this ranked first (or second, third) line listing every point it earned, for example solid materials +2; flame spread +2; ventilation +2; variable-flow coverage +1. The score is research relevance only, not fire risk. Under each result:

ControlWhat it does
Open NASA PSI record / Original PSI CSVOpens NASA's own page, or downloads the exact original table.
Compare (tick box)Selects the experiment for side-by-side comparison. Two can be selected at a time.
All documented conditions & measurement notesExpands the detail: conditions, notes and the AstroFlare interpretation, clearly separated.
Evidence types, selected rows and limitsShows which rows were selected and what the data cannot tell you.

3. The NASA research explorer (continued)

3.4 What should we test next?

This panel turns the top result into a proposed follow-up, in three parts: What NASA measured, What's missing for this scenario and Next research step. It is AstroFlare's proposal, not a NASA recommendation. Open Variable and fixed conditions for the proposed study to see what to change and what to hold fixed.

Copy research brief + citations copies the whole panel with the original NASA citations to your clipboard. If your browser blocks clipboard access, a text box appears with the brief already selected, so you can copy it by hand.

3.5 Compare investigations side by side

Press View comparison (2) to jump here. The table has one column per experiment and these rows: Study match / curated run match; Material / fuel; Gravity regime; Oxygen concentration; Airflow; Reported outcome (evidence type labelled); NASA source. Reported measurements, published qualitative descriptions and AstroFlare's interpretation are kept in separate rows so you can tell which is which. Use Remove BASS-II or Remove Saffire II to take one out, then tick another result to replace it. A third selection is refused with a message.

3.6 Ask about the evidence

Type a question in Your evidence question and press Find cited answer (or press Enter). Three example questions are provided as buttons: What was the flame spread rate in Saffire II? / What flame spread rate was measured in lunar gravity? / What was the flame spread rate in Saffire I?

You ask about...You get
A named measurement in a named experiment (for example Saffire II spread rate)The value, its conditions, and the NASA table, file and row. Example: 2.1 mm/s for run 2-5, 20 cm/s concurrent flow.
Something no experiment measured (for example lunar-gravity flame spread)An honest gap: the curated studies were in microgravity and give no lunar measurement.
A field the table does not report (for example Saffire I spread rate in its table)A statement that it is not reported, plus NASA's companion report values where they exist, labelled as such.
Safety limits, predictions, certification or a value at new conditionsA refusal to invent a number. Nothing is predicted or extrapolated.
Two experiments at onceA request to ask about one experiment at a time.

3.7 Explain with AI (optional)

Under an answer, Explain with AI (optional) asks for a short plain-language explanation based only on the curated NASA records. While it works, a line says Writing an explanation from the curated NASA records. The result is shown with the label "Automatically generated explanation ... Not written or reviewed by a person; checked for consistency, not scientific correctness" and the records it used. The answer above it never depends on this step.

  • "An explanation was produced but withheld": a safety check rejected it. Press Explain again with AI, or rely on the cited answer above.
  • "The explanation service did not return a usable result": the free AI is busy or unavailable. Try again in a minute.

3.8 Measurements and methodology

Explore actual measurements: BASS-II carbon monoxide readings shows a bar chart of the final carbon monoxide readings for three runs, with initial readings and conditions. These are gas concentrations, not fire probabilities or safe exposure limits. Methodology, sources and limitations explains the ranking rule and links to the downloadable data set and the full documentation.

4. Mission screening, step by step

Describe a mission, answer guided questions, and get cited findings and a report.

Stage 1: Mission

Type a description in What mission are you assessing? (up to 2,000 characters). Three example descriptions are offered as links you can click to try the tool. Then choose:

ControlWhat it does
Read my descriptionReads your text with keyword rules (and the optional AI) and shows what it understood. It does not move on until you confirm.
Skip: I'll answer the questions myselfGoes straight to the questions without reading any text.
Use the ticked items and continueCarries the ticked items forward as answers.

Reading your description

After Read my description, a list titled What AstroFlare understood appears. Each item has a tick box, the value it found, a tag, and the exact words it came from. The tag Stated means your words said it directly. Inferred means AstroFlare guessed from context (for example "transit" suggests microgravity); inferred items start unticked, and each says what to confirm. Tick only what is right. Nothing is used until you tick it. When ready, press Use the ticked items and continue.

If the optional AI is available, it may add proposals or up to three clarifying questions. Every AI proposal must quote your own words and passes the same checks as typed answers.

Stage 2: Problem

Choose one hazard and one goal, then Continue.

ChoiceMeaning
Electrical overheating or arcHeating or arcing in wiring and electrical equipment. Gives a fault current integral (I squared t) when fault data are supplied.
Battery thermal eventRecords the reported stored-energy situation only; it does not model cell chemistry or thermal propagation.
Cabin material ignitionDepends on how far flammability tests cover your atmosphere and gravity.
Persistent smoke or combustion productsDetection, ventilation and clean-up provisions after a fire.
Goal: Investigate a specific hazardFull screening: asks about the environment, the equipment and the safeguards, then reports conditions that warrant review.
Goal: Find out what information is missingA short path: only the hazard and the cabin environment are asked. Everything else is reported as missing, with why it matters.
Goal: See which answers matter mostFull screening, then a check of how each answer changes the findings and the heuristic index when varied.

4. Mission screening (continued)

Stage 3: Information

Questions come in small groups, one group per page, headed Step n of N. For the electrical hazard the groups are: Cabin environment; Mission context; The circuit; Faults and protection; Airflow; Detection, suppression and response; Materials and other ignition sources. Other hazards ask a shorter or different set, and some questions are skipped depending on earlier answers (the page tells you why).

Each question has:

ControlWhat it does
Why it mattersOne line on why AstroFlare asks, so you can judge whether to answer.
The question and its unitsFor example kPa or psia for pressure; units are shown and converted for you.
I don't knowRecords the answer as unknown instead of guessing. Unknowns are reported as gaps, with why they matter.
How do you know this?Basis: not stated; Specification or design document; Test or measurement; Analysis or simulation; My estimate; Working assumption.
Be honest about how you know. Choosing a weak basis is fine and is useful: it is reported in the limitations rather than hidden. Choosing "Specification" for something you only assumed would make the result look stronger than it is. A provision marked "verified" with only an estimate behind it is flagged as unverified.

Press Continue after each group, or Back to revise the previous one. A blank answer is treated as unknown. Nothing is ever defaulted.

Stage 4: Review

A profile lists what you entered, which answers are estimates or assumptions, which are unknown, the values AstroFlare derived from your answers, and the assumptions it introduced. Any row can be edited without losing the others. Where estimates, assumptions or unknowns exist you must acknowledge them before continuing. Then press Run the assessment.

Stage 5: Results

Results are read top to bottom. At the top: Edit answers, Pin as baseline and Prepare report, with counts of calculations, cited statements and a note that independent validation and literature search were not performed.

SectionWhat it shows
Assessment overviewYour screening question, the scenario in words, the result in one paragraph (for example: how many conditions warrant review and how many information gaps limit the assessment), and the evidence limits.
Missing answers and evidence limitsUnanswered input fields, and the limits of the scientific evidence behind the findings.
Key findingsGrouped as a hazard pathway, then conditions warranting review. Each finding has Why this matters, Reasoning, and Limitations and competing explanations, and can show its cited statements.
Open source SxxOpens the source behind a statement: what it establishes, how deeply it was read, and a link to NASA's record.
Show the reasoning and inputsExpands exactly which of your answers led to the finding.
Generate explanation for this missionOptional AI paragraph summarising the results in plain language, labelled as unreviewed.

4. Mission screening (continued)

Investigate further

Below the findings, Investigate further runs real calculations on your assessment. Each result is added to a log underneath, so you can compare answers as you go. Six buttons:

ButtonAnswers
Which answer matters most?Re-runs the assessment with each choice-type answer changed in turn; shows how far the heuristic index moves and which findings appear or disappear.
What information is missing?Lists every unanswered input and why it matters.
What should I verify before relying on this?Lists answers that rest on estimates, assumptions or no stated basis.
Show all calculationsEvery calculation with formula, inputs, assumptions and limits.
What do the cited sources establish?What each cited NASA statement does and does not support.
How is the heuristic index built?The exact points behind the single index, and why it is not a probability.

Try a different answer

Pick an input from the list Input to change, give a new value, and press Compare. AstroFlare re-runs the assessment with only that answer changed and shows what moved. The changed input carries no stated basis in this comparison.

Pin as baseline

Press Pin as baseline to remember the current assessment. After you edit answers and run again, the page can show what changed between the two. Use it to see how closing an information gap changes the findings.

Ask a question about this assessment

Type a question and press Ask (or Enter). Questions about influence, missing information, calculations, sources and verification are answered by the built-in logic. Other questions may go to the optional AI, which may only answer from this assessment, must cite real findings or sources, and may say it cannot answer.

Stage 6: Report

Press Prepare report. The report page offers:

ControlWhat it does
Download report (Markdown)A readable report file you can open in any text editor.
Download data (JSON)The same assessment as structured data for other tools.
PrintPrints the report, or saves it as a PDF from your browser's print dialog.
Back to resultsReturns to the results without losing anything.

The report contains the mission and objective, every input with its basis, values AstroFlare derived, calculations with formulas, findings with reasoning, the evidence used, missing information, limitations, next steps, the date, and a disclosure of whether any text was automatically generated.

5. Reading results correctly

The most important skill in using AstroFlare is knowing what a result does and does not mean.

Finding kindMeaningWhat to do with it
Plausible pathwayA way a fire could start for the hazard you chose. Never a demonstrated failure.Use it as an agenda item for review.
Condition warranting reviewSomething in your answers a safety team should examine, for example oxygen above Earth air or ventilation that cannot be stopped.Check the reasoning, the limits and the cited source, then decide who must look at it.
Information gapSomething needed that you did not supply.Gather it, then edit your answers and run again.
ContextA relevant fact that is not a concern on its own, for example mission duration.Read it; no action implied.

What not to conclude

  • "Few findings" does not mean "safe". It may only mean you supplied few answers. Check Missing answers first.
  • A higher index is not higher risk. The index is a developer-chosen heuristic, uncalibrated, comparable only for the same hazard, and shown only when every needed input was supplied.
  • An experiment in microgravity is not a lunar test. The research explorer flags this on every result.
  • "Not reported" is not zero. It means the source table did not give a value.
  • Automatically generated text can be wrong. It is checked for unsupported numbers and forbidden claims, not for subtle mistakes. Read the cited answer, not only the explanation.

Good habits

  • Describe the mission in your own words first; let AstroFlare show you what it understood, and correct it.
  • Prefer "I don't know" to a guess. A gap is information; a wrong number is not.
  • State the basis honestly for every number.
  • Open at least one cited source for each finding you intend to act on.
  • Pin a baseline before changing answers, so you can see what each change did.
  • Hand the report to a qualified fire-safety specialist; it is designed as their starting point.

6. AI features

AI is optional, labelled, and checked. Everything it appears beside works without it.

WhereControlWhat it does
Mission stageRead my descriptionAlso asks the AI for proposals; each must quote your words and still needs your tick.
Results stageGenerate explanation for this missionA short mission-specific explanation of the results.
Investigate stageAsk (for unusual questions)An answer limited to this assessment, citing real findings or sources.
Research explorerExplain with AI (optional)A short explanation limited to the curated NASA records, citing the records used.

What you will see

  • Text from AI always carries a label such as "Automatically generated ... not written or reviewed by a person".
  • If the AI is busy or off, a single plain sentence says so and nothing else changes.
  • If a reply fails the safety checks it is withheld and you are told so. You can try again or ignore it.
  • The site shares a limited free AI quota between all visitors, so heavy use in a short time may show "busy". Wait a minute and retry.

What is sent to the AI

Only when you press an AI-backed control, and only the fields described: for evidence explanations, your question and the curated NASA records; for mission features, the structured results and the mission description you typed. Never your whole form, never anything from other visitors, and the site stores nothing about you.

7. Saving, sharing and privacy

QuestionAnswer
Where are my answers kept?In the open page in your browser. If you press Save in this browser they are stored on this device only; they are never uploaded.
Does AstroFlare store my mission?No. The server is stateless and keeps no mission data; logs record only the type of request and its result.
Can others see my work?No. Nothing is shared unless you download or copy it yourself.
Can I move my work to another device?Download the data (JSON) report, or re-enter your answers; saved answers do not leave the browser they were saved in.
What if I clear my browser data?Saved answers are removed with it. Download a report first if you need a copy.

Sharing your results

Use Download report (Markdown) or Print to share a report. To share explorer evidence, use Copy research brief + citations. The data set and the original NASA tables can be downloaded from the explorer's methodology section, and the entire project source from the site footer.

8. Troubleshooting

ProblemTry this
The page is slow the first timeThe free service wakes from sleep. Wait about a minute; later visits are quick.
The page says explanations are unavailableThe optional AI is off or busy. Everything else works; try again later.
"An explanation was produced but withheld"The safety checks rejected the reply. Press the AI button again or use the cited answer.
I cannot press ContinueA required answer is missing or invalid. The page names the field; fix it or choose I don't know.
A value is rejectedIt is outside the allowed range or the wrong unit. Check the unit shown beside the field.
Results look emptyFew inputs were supplied. Open "Missing answers and evidence limits"; fewer answers means fewer findings, not a safer design.
A filter shows no resultsPress Reset experiment filters; some filters require explicitly documented values.
Copy brief did not copyYour browser blocked the clipboard. The brief appears in a text box; select and copy it manually.
My saved answers will not loadThe saved data was damaged or invalid. Current answers are left unchanged; re-enter them or start over.
Tables are cut off on my phoneSwipe sideways inside the table; the page itself does not scroll sideways.
